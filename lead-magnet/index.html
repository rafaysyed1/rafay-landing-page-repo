Below is a short piece of Day 1 so you can see how practical it is. Try it before your next walk.

From Day 1

If your dog launches out the door

  1. Clip the leash on with the door still closed. Hold a treat in your hand.
  2. Open the door a few inches. If your dog pushes forward, close it gently and wait.
  3. When your dog backs off or looks at you, say "yes," give the treat by your leg, and open the door again.

Most dogs get this in 5 to 10 tries. It feels slow the first morning. By day three, a lot of owners tell me the door is the calmest part of the walk.

The same simple rule runs through all five days.

A tight leash pauses the walk. A loose leash keeps it going.

Your dog pulls because pulling works. It gets them to the smell, the other dog or the park a little faster.

Over five days, you change that deal. When the leash goes tight, you stop. When it goes loose, you walk again. Dogs learn fast when the rule stays the same every single time.

Who this may not suit

The reset is for everyday pulling, like the excited dog who just wants to get somewhere faster.

If your dog lunges at people or other dogs, has bitten, or pulls hard enough to knock you off balance, please work with a qualified trainer in person first.

The course gives general training tips. It can't assess behavior or health problems. If the pulling started suddenly, check with your vet.
