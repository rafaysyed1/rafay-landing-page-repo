"I'm a tester. I'm not a coder."

I said that sentence for three years. I bought two coding courses and quit both by week two. They started with variables and loops, and the examples were shopping lists and pizza orders. None of it looked like my job, so none of it stuck.

Those courses treated me as a beginner at everything. I was a beginner at code, sure. But I'd been writing test cases for years, and a test case already holds most of what an automated test needs.

Once I saw that, I learned in a different order. I call it Steps-to-Spec.

The Steps-to-Spec method

Look at any test case you wrote this month. It has steps, test data and an expected result. That's about 70% of a Playwright test, and you've been writing that part for years.

Already in your test case: steps, data, expected result
Missing
0%70%100%
Locators
How the code finds the button you would click.
Assertions
How the code checks your expected result.
Waits
How the code knows the page is ready.

The missing 30% comes down to those three things. Greenrun teaches them first, using test cases like yours. The rest of the syntax comes later, when a step needs it.

One test a night

I joined QA straight out of university, on the payments team at a mid-size fintech. I spent four years there, all of it manual. I was good at the job. I found bugs other people missed, because I read every requirement twice and asked a lot of questions.

Then we got a new team lead. In her first sprint retro, she asked why regression took three days. Nobody had a good answer.

So I started small, with one test a night after work, built from my own test cases. The first one took me a whole week. By month three, I could write five in an evening. By the end of that year, our suite had 300 tests, and regression ran in 40 minutes on every pull request.

Today I'm a Senior SDET, and our suite has 1,300 tests. Since 2022, I've taught 2,100 manual testers to make the same move in less time.

Questions testers ask

Do I need to know JavaScript?

No. You'll learn the parts of TypeScript that tests use, as you need them. Module 1 assumes you've never written code.

Why Playwright and not Selenium?

Both are good tools. Playwright waits for the page by itself and runs three browsers out of the box. It also shows up in more new job posts. If your company uses Selenium, the ideas carry over.

Why TypeScript and not Python?

Playwright supports both. Most Playwright job posts ask for TypeScript or JavaScript, so that's what we teach.

How long does it take?

About 4 hours a week for 8 weeks. Some students go faster. You keep access for life, so a slower pace is fine too.

Does it work on Windows?

Yes. Windows, Mac and Linux all work, and Lesson 1.2 covers each one.

Can my company pay?

Yes. Send the manager letter. Checkout gives you a proper invoice with your company's name on it.

What if I get stuck?

Post in the community. Sana or a teaching assistant replies within one working day.

Do you guarantee a job?

No. We guarantee you'll write passing tests, or you get your money back. The job search is yours, and Pro gives you tools for it.
